Compare your restaurant to the benchmark

Enter one year of your numbers. The page works out your margin, compares every cost line with the average Canadian restaurant, and shows what a 1% cut is worth to you. It runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.

Gap = what you would save (or lose) by spending the benchmark share of your revenue on that line. Red means you spend more than the average restaurant. A gap is a question to look into, not proof of waste: a full-service place with table staff should pay more labour than a counter-service one.

How it works

  • Benchmark shares come from the ISED Canadian Industry Statistics profile of NAICS 7225, 65,071 restaurants, 2024: cost of sales 44.8%, labour 23.6%, rent 8.4%, utilities 2.5%, amortization 2.7%, other 15.4% and net profit 2.5%. "Other" is a calculated line (revenue minus every listed cost and profit), so treat that row with care.
  • Break-even revenue treats cost of sales as the only cost that moves with sales and every other cost as fixed: fixed costs divided by (1 minus cost of sales share). This is a simplification, since labour is partly variable.
  • 42.3% of restaurants in the same data lost money in 2024, and the bottom quarter averaged a $13,000 loss.
