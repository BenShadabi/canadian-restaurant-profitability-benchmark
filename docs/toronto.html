Read this first. The national benchmark on the project page comes from Statistics Canada and ISED financial data. Nobody publishes profit for a Toronto neighbourhood, so this page does not estimate it. It only counts licences: how many there are per resident, and how many ended. A licence ending is not always a failed restaurant (see the limits below).

How the neighbourhoods compare

Most licences per resident, and the lowest and highest shares of licences that ended (only neighbourhoods with 50 or more licences). Yonge-Bay Corridor has the most per resident, 20.9 per 1,000 against 2.8 for the whole city. Downtown cores rank high partly because office workers and visitors eat there but do not live there. Among the 47 rankable neighbourhoods the share that ended runs from 4.7% (Clairlea-Birchmount) to 22.2% (Wellington Place), but with 50 to 250 licences each a few cases move these numbers, so treat gaps of a few points as noise.

Limits

Not profit.

There is no income, rent or cost data here. The 2.5% net margin on the main page is a national average and is not split by area.

A cancelled licence is not always a closure.

I only count a cancellation as a closure when no other licence starts at the same address and unit within 90 days. That still misses some owner changes and any restaurant replaced within 90 days. Use the share to compare areas, not as a true closure rate (the raw cancellation share was 31%).

Placing licences is approximate.

The licence file has no coordinates. 8,829 of 10,819 were placed by matching the street address to a DineSafe health-inspection premises, 1,728 at the average position of premises sharing their postal code (they can land in a neighbouring area), and 262 could not be placed and are left out.

Only one licence type.

I count "Eating or drinking establishment" licences. Take-out and retail food licences, food trucks and sidewalk cafes are separate categories and are not counted. Bars are included with restaurants.

Old population data.

Residents come from the 2021 census, and some neighbourhoods have grown a lot since.

Most neighbourhoods are too small to rank.

111 of 158 have fewer than 50 licences, so their closure shares are not shown on the map or in the rankings.

How I built it

  • Python (pandas, shapely, matplotlib) reads City of Toronto datasets and writes small CSVs to the data folder: toronto_neighbourhoods.csv, toronto_bia.csv and toronto_match_summary.csv. The raw files are about 80 MB and are downloaded by a script rather than stored.
  • Licences come from the Municipal Licensing and Standards business licences file (snapshot taken 21 October 2026). Coordinates come from DineSafe. Boundaries are the City's 158 neighbourhoods and its BIAs. Population is the 2021 Neighbourhood Profiles.
  • This page is plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, with the map drawn as inline SVG. The code is analysis/toronto_download.py, build_toronto.py, build_toronto_charts.py and build_toronto_page.py, and checks.py checks that the numbers on this page match the CSVs.
