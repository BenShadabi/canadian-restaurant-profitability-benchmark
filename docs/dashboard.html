    Three cost levers would take the average restaurant from $21,500 to $68,169 a year

    What-if on the 2024 average restaurant. Annual profit in dollars. Hover or tap a bar for the running total.

    Why it matters: profit is only 2.5% of revenue, so a small cost change is a big profit change. A 35% profit increase needs about $7,500 a year, which is a 3.8% labour cut on its own.

    • Labour -10%: +$20,020
    • Overhead (utilities, telecom, other) -15%: +$22,845
    • Food waste -20%: +$3,804

    Assumption: I took food waste as 5% of cost of sales. That share is my assumption, not source data. Overhead also includes a calculated remainder of expenses, so the conservative case below leaves it out. This is a what-if, not a forecast.

    How to read these numbers

    • The two sources use different bases. ISED reports net profit for small restaurants (2.5% margin). Statistics Canada reports operating profit for the whole industry (4.1%). I only compare lines within one source.
    • Full-service plus limited-service revenue is less than the industry total because drinking places and other food services are not shown ($89.1B vs $99.6B in 2024). Some 2019 and 2022 figures come from later releases that compare back to those years. Blank margins were not published.
    • The what-if applies cuts to an average restaurant. It does not describe any one business.
